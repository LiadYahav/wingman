# Upgrade: 3.7 → 3.8 (add-on authoring; day2 writes per team)

For an agent inside the disconnected environment. Follow the steps in order.
**STOP** means: change nothing more, finish the report (step 7) and hand it
back.

`<ns>`, `<release>`, `<tag>` and `$CHART` mean what they mean in
[3.3-to-3.4.md](3.3-to-3.4.md); `<tag>` is `3.8.0` or a later 3.8 release.

## 1. What changes

| Area | 3.7 | 3.8 |
|---|---|---|
| Add-ons | Install, update, remove on clusters | Also: a team creates a new add-on (`operators/<chart>/`), edits its team default (deploy config, sync options, values), makes it a fleet default (every hosted cluster, every MCE, the hub, every UPI cluster) and edits fleet defaults. One MR on the team's repo each (ADR-0043) |
| Who can write | Every team, as far as GitLab let Wingman's account | **Per team.** A team whose project gives Wingman's GitLab account Developer or higher is writable; any other is read-only in Wingman, with the reason shown, and every day2 write for it is refused (`403 TEAM_READ_ONLY`). Reads are unchanged |
| Chart, Secrets, settings | — | No new value, Secret or variable |
| Metrics | — | The GitLab `operation` label gains `access_level` (`docs/ops/metrics.md`) |

If Wingman's account is Developer (or higher) on the sigs group, every team
stays writable and nothing changes in behaviour: do steps 3 and 5 only. The
rest is for teams that should not, or do not, give Wingman write access.

## 2. What to bring in from the connected side

The three images of `<tag>` and the repository at `v<tag>`, as for 3.7.

## 3. Discover (read-only)

In GitLab's web UI, as someone who can see members (no token in any output):

1. The sigs group (the chart value `gitlab.day2SigsGroupPath`) → **Members**: is
   Wingman's account there, directly or from a group above it, and with which
   role?
2. Each team project `sigs/<team>` → **Members**: Wingman's account's role
   there, if any.
3. Ask the platform team which teams want Wingman to write to their repo.

Record the three answers. If Wingman's account is a GitLab instance admin with
no memberships: **STOP**. 3.8 reads its role from memberships, so every team
would read as having no access; the account must be a member.

## 4. Decide and change the memberships (GitLab, not Wingman)

- **Every team writable** (as before): Developer on the sigs group. Nothing to
  change if that is what step 3 found.
- **Per team**: Reporter on the sigs group (so every team's pages load), and
  Developer on each `sigs/<team>` that opts in. A Developer membership on the
  group would make every team writable again: lower it to Reporter. A
  maintainer of each opting-in project adds the account there.

Changing a membership is done by the group's or project's owners, not by this
upgrade's operator, unless they are the same person: if you may not change
them, record the decision and go on; Wingman shows each team as it finds it.

## 5. Upgrade

The generic upgrade of [README.md](README.md#the-generic-upgrade-no-guide),
with `<tag>`.

## 6. Verify

1. Open Wingman → Add-ons. Each team that should be writable shows its
   **New add-on** button enabled; each other team shows as read-only, with
   the sentence naming its project and the account's role.
2. Open a writable team's add-on → **Make default** → pick a scope →
   **Preview**. The preview lists the files and the clusters it reaches, and
   opens nothing. Close it.
3. A membership changed in step 4 shows within `DAY2_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS`
   (15 s by default) plus a few seconds for GitLab.

A team you expected writable shows read-only: check its project's Members
(step 3.2) before anything else. Don't change Wingman to work around it.

## 7. Roll back, and report

`helm rollback <release> <revision> -n <ns>` restores 3.7: Wingman keeps no
state of its own. Membership changes are GitLab's and stay as they are; under
3.7 a team without Developer simply gets GitLab's refusal on a write.

Report: step 3's findings, the decision of step 4 and who changed what, the
helm revision before and after, and step 6's results.
